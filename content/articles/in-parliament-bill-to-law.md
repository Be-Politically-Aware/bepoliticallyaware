# How does a Bill become an enforceable law?

A Bill is a legislative proposal. An Act is an enacted law. To understand what changed, identify the exact text and the stage it has reached rather than relying on a headline saying a law was “approved”.

Rajya Sabha's official introduction explains the general process from Bill to enactment. The Constitution supplies the rules and exceptions that prevent this from being a single identical path for every Bill. [1](https://sansad.in/rs/legislation/introduction) [2](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=79)

## Follow the text, not only the title

For an ordinary parliamentary Bill, check introduction, consideration and passage in the Houses, including any amendments. Do not assume the version introduced is identical to the version passed.

A parliamentary committee may examine a Bill, but do not claim every Bill automatically receives the same committee process. Use the record for that particular proposal. [3](https://sansad.in/ls/faq)

If a headline says a Bill cleared one House, look for the other House's position and the applicable procedure. A party announcement or Cabinet decision is not a substitute for the parliamentary record.

## Special categories need their own rules

Money Bills have a defined constitutional meaning and a special procedure under Articles 109 and 110. Rajya Sabha's role includes recommendations within a 14-day period, which Lok Sabha may accept or reject. A Bill is not a Money Bill merely because its proposal would cost money. [4](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=80)

Do not apply that shortcut to an ordinary Bill or describe all financial legislation as though it necessarily follows the same procedure.

## Check assent and commencement separately

After the required legislative stages, check the assent and published Act. Then read its commencement clause. The date an Act receives assent and the date a particular provision comes into operation need not be the same; the Act may specify dates or require a notification. For example, section 1(3) of the original BNSS text provides for commencement by Central Government notification; this is an illustration of the distinction, not a claim about a new commencement event today. [5](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=2)

For an actual current-law claim, retrieve the relevant notification, later amendment or order affecting operation. This article explains how to read the stages; it does not declare a named Bill currently enforceable.

## A hypothetical headline audit

“New rules apply from today” could be wrong even if the underlying Act has received assent. Ask which provision creates the rule, which commencement instrument applies and whether the announcement is reporting an effective rule or a future proposal.

The safe summary is exact: “The Bill passed this House on this date” or “this provision commenced under this notification”. Precision makes it harder to present an incomplete stage as a finished change in the law.
