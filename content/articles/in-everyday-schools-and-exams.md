A school may appear well supplied in a national summary while a particular class lacks a teacher. An examination announcement may describe a process without explaining why a candidate’s result is delayed. Start with the institution and the specific failure, not the assumption that one national office operates everything.

Identify who runs the school or examination, who employs the staff and which authority issued the relevant rule or notice. India’s constitutional allocation of legislative subjects is a starting point, not a substitute for finding the actual operating authority. [1](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=176)

## What the latest inspected school release reports

The Ministry of Education’s release of **7 July 2026**, covering **UDISE+ 2025–26**, reports **10,273,020 teachers** and **100,843 single-teacher schools**. These are different kinds of totals: a larger national workforce does not establish that each school has the staff it needs. [2](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282141&lang=1&reg=48)

The same release reports computer access in 69.9% of schools and internet access in 67.4%. A reported facility is not, by itself, evidence of reliable connectivity, functioning equipment or regular classroom use. Those require more specific checks. [3](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282141&lang=1&reg=48)

The accompanying data card reproduces selected ministry tables, not an independent inspection of schools or a full extraction of the national report.

## Read the table when the narrative sounds too smooth

One paragraph in the release describes a consistent decline in dropout across all levels. Its table is more specific: secondary-stage dropout fell from **8.2% in 2024–25 to 7.0% in 2025–26**, while middle-stage dropout rose from **3.5% to 3.6%**. Those changes are minus 1.2 and plus 0.1 percentage points respectively. [4](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282141&lang=1&reg=48)

This does not establish the cause of either change, or justify declaring the whole system a success or failure. It does show why an article should preserve the stage, year and table values rather than repeat a broad summary uncritically.

These tables use NEP-stage labels. Do not silently join them to an older table using different school-stage definitions.

## Enrolment is not learning

School administrative records and household learning surveys have different coverage. ASER’s basic design is a rural household survey: it records enrolment for children aged 3–16 and assesses basic reading and arithmetic for ages 5–16. It can include children outside school; it is not an urban-and-rural census of every pupil. [5](https://asercentre.org/aser-survey/)

Consequently, a school-facility percentage and an ASER learning estimate cannot be treated as measurements of the same thing. Neither should be used to make a claim beyond its population and method.

## Investigate the problem at its actual level

For a teacher shortage, useful questions concern sanctioned posts, filled posts, subject allocation and the dated timetable. For unusable toilets or equipment, ask about inspection and maintenance records, not just installation counts.

For an examination or recruitment problem, keep the original notification, timetable, result notice and any published corrigendum together. Distinguish an allegation of a leak from a documented finding, cancellation or court order. Verify current challenge procedures from that authority before relying on a forwarded deadline.

Protect pupils’ personal information. A public accountability question can usually be framed around staffing, facilities or procedure without publishing a child’s name, marks or identity documents.
