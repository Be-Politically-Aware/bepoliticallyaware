A person being accused, arrested, charged or convicted does not describe the same legal status. A responsible account names the event that actually occurred and links the relevant record.

The following are introductory descriptions, not advice about an individual case. Procedure can depend on the proceeding and applicable law. The BNSS itself includes savings for specified proceedings pending under the earlier criminal procedure code, so an old case should not automatically be relabelled as a new-code case. [1](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=156)

## From allegation to a case before a court

An **allegation** is a claim that someone did something. Calling it an allegation does not establish that it is true or false.

An **FIR**, or First Information Report, records information in the criminal-law process concerning a cognizable offence. It is not a judgment of guilt. **Arrest** concerns taking a person into custody; it does not itself decide the accusation. [2](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=52)

A **police report or chargesheet** follows investigation in the relevant procedural setting. Do not confuse it with a court's decision to frame a charge. The BNSS separately provides for a police report on completion of investigation and for court procedures concerning discharge and framing charges. [3](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=62) [4](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=82)

A **court-framed charge** identifies the accusation to be tried at that stage. It is not a conviction.

## Release is not a verdict

**Bail** concerns release from custody subject to the applicable conditions and bond arrangement. A bail order is not an acquittal. Read the actual order rather than assuming that release proves either innocence or political favour. [5](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=2)

## Read the outcome precisely

A **conviction** is a judicial finding of guilt. An **acquittal** is a judgment finding the accused not guilty in the proceeding. [6](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=83) A **discharge** is a different procedural outcome, illustrated by the BNSS provisions dealing with whether sufficient grounds exist to proceed before framing a charge. [4](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/250884_2_english_01042024.pdf#page=82)

An **appeal** challenges a decision; filing it does not tell you its outcome. If an order stays a sentence or conviction, identify exactly what was stayed. Do not collapse every stay, discharge, withdrawal or quashing into the word “acquittal”.

## Preserve the case identity

Record the court, case number, relevant charge or proceeding, order date and current status date. Separate multiple proceedings involving the same person. Include a material later acquittal, reversal or correction where an earlier allegation was reported.

A historic election affidavit is not a substitute for this timeline. Nor does party membership establish political motive. Where the documents do not establish motive or current status, say so rather than filling in the gap.
