# Who is actually responsible for this local problem?

The nearest public office is not necessarily the office responsible for your problem. Begin with the service or asset: this road, this drain, this water connection, this school.

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj's Local Government Directory can help identify administrative units and rural or urban local bodies. It does not establish ownership of every public asset or the legal duty to fix it. [1](https://panchayat.gov.in/en/lgd/)

## Build a responsibility trail

Record the location precisely enough for an office to identify it. Look for an official asset number, service bill, public notice, work sign or department page. Treat these as leads to verify, especially when a sign is old.

Then separate **owner**, **operator** and **maintenance agency**. They may be the same body, but do not assume that they are. Ask which record establishes responsibility: an asset register, maintenance contract, transfer or handover order, or the relevant service terms.

## Local does not always mean municipal

The Constitution's Twelfth Schedule includes roads, water and sanitation among municipal subjects, while Article 243W ties the actual conferral of powers to state legislation. The schedule is not proof that your municipality operates a particular highway, water network or special-area service. [2](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=162) [3](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=381)

A **hypothetical example**: residents report a broken road to a councillor. The office says another agency owns it. The next useful step is to ask for the agency's name and the record or reference supporting that answer, rather than repeatedly forwarding the same complaint without a recipient.

## Confirm the channel before sharing information

Use the institution's official website or a verified official directory to locate its current public contact or grievance route. Do not assume that an email address in a forwarded poster is authentic.

Keep a private copy of your full submission. A public version should remove phone numbers, identity documents and details about other people that are unnecessary to establish the problem.

## When responsibility remains disputed

Document each reply, including dates and reference numbers. Ask a narrow follow-up: “Does your office maintain this asset? If not, which record identifies the responsible body?” A request for existing records and a request to repair a service may need different routes.

Do not turn an unresolved routing dispute into a confident allegation that a named person deliberately refused a duty you have not established.

## Try this

Create a responsibility card: location, service or asset, owner, operator, supporting record, official complaint channel and unresolved questions.
