A minister’s answer can tell you what the government records, what it plans to do, or what it cannot currently provide. The useful material is often a short answer or an attached table, not the most widely shared speech.

## What question formats do

A **starred question** seeks an oral answer; an **unstarred question** receives a written answer. Lok Sabha also has short-notice questions for urgent matters and questions addressed to private members about matters for which they are responsible. These formats have different procedures. A starred question appearing on a list does not establish that it was actually reached orally. [1](https://sansad.in/ls/faq)

Debates provide another kind of record: members’ arguments, government responses and, where applicable, decisions. A speech is evidence of what the speaker said. Its factual claims still need their own support. Parliamentary status does not turn every sentence in a debate into a verified finding.

## Keep the question separate from the reply

Read a question’s numbered parts before the answer. A question may contain an allegation or an assumed figure. Unless the response confirms it, do not report that premise as a government admission.

A concrete example is **Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 2110, answered on 12 December 2025**, about the Pre-Legislative Consultation Policy. The answer said that the policy’s effectiveness had not been evaluated and that the Ministry did not maintain records of compliance. Those are narrower statements than “the government never consults anyone”. [2](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/186/AU2110_008KJB.pdf?source=pqals#page=1)

The original record is useful precisely because it reveals the boundary of the answer. Preserve the date, the ministry and the wording of the missing information. A later answer could change the picture; a 2025 reply should not be silently relabelled the government’s position today.

## Follow the annexure

**Hypothetical example:** an MP asks about doctor vacancies in a district. The answer supplies statewide sanctioned posts, then points to an annexure. Before using the figures, check whether the annexure distinguishes sanctioned, filled and vacant posts; whether it covers your district; and the date on which the positions were counted.

An answer about recruitment advertisements would not, by itself, establish how many doctors actually joined. An answer about the entire state would not establish the position in one hospital. Record the mismatch instead of substituting a convenient number.

## Find a record that someone else can reopen

Search the relevant House’s questions section by ministry, subject and answer date. Keep the question number, House, session or date, and the original download link. For a debate, record the sitting date and the relevant part of the proceedings. Do not rely on a screenshot with the header removed.

Then make a small evidence note: what was asked, what was answered, which table supports it, and what remains missing. These are suggested research steps, not a guarantee that every search interface will expose every record in the same way.

Question counts can show activity, but they cannot tell you whether the questions were useful, whether answers were adequate, or whether a failure was corrected. Read a sample of the actual work before treating a large count as a performance verdict.
