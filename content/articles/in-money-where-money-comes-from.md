When someone says a programme is “paid for by taxpayers”, ask for the fuller financing picture. A budget combines different kinds of receipts, and some create obligations for later years.

## Start with the budget’s categories

The Union’s Budget at a Glance distinguishes tax and non-tax revenue within **revenue receipts**. Its capital-receipts section includes recovery of loans, other receipts, and borrowings and other liabilities. Borrowing is therefore a receipt in this table, but it is not tax revenue. [1](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=1) [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

The distinction matters when reading a pie chart. It also matters when someone claims that total receipts show the government has “earned” enough to cover expenditure. A table that includes borrowing balances financing sources against uses; it does not show that spending is covered entirely by non-debt income.

## A worked Union Budget example

For **FY 2026–27 Budget Estimates**, the main table reports revenue receipts of **₹35,33,150 crore**: net tax revenue to the Centre of **₹28,66,922 crore**, plus non-tax revenue of **₹6,66,228 crore**. It separately lists **₹16,95,768 crore** under borrowings and other liabilities. These are estimates for that financial year, not collections already completed. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

Check the addition: 28,66,922 + 6,66,228 = 35,33,150. That reproduces the revenue-receipts subtotal. Do not add interest payments here: interest is an expenditure item, not a separate source of receipts.

The borrowing line’s note includes cash-balance drawdown. Relabelling the entire line “fresh market borrowing” would change its meaning. Keep the published category intact when exporting it to a chart. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

## Whose government finances?

This table concerns the **Union**. It is not a combined statement of all state and local governments. “Tax revenue net to Centre” is also different from a gross collection figure that includes amounts shared with states. The Budget’s “Rupee Comes From” presentation includes states’ tax share, so it should not be spliced into the main net-receipts table without checking the denominator. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3) [3](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=4)

**Hypothetical example:** one graphic shows a tax category as a share of gross receipts and another as a share of revenue retained by a government. Different percentages could result even with the same underlying tax figure. The denominator is part of the claim.

## A receipt can have consequences beyond this year

Recovering a loan is not the same event as charging a tax. Selling an asset is not the same as receiving a recurring payment indefinitely. Borrowing now leaves a different financing question for the future. Evaluate the category and its implications instead of declaring every increase in receipts automatically good or bad.

Useful scrutiny asks which assumption supports an estimate, whether receipts materialised, and how the spending they finance is performing. A household analogy can illustrate arithmetic, but should not replace the actual institutions and accounting categories in the budget.

When you share a figure, include the government level, financial year, BE/RE/Actual label and units. Those four details prevent many misleading comparisons before an argument even begins.
