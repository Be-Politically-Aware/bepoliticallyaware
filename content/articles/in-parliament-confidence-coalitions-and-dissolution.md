News about a government “losing its majority” can refer to several different things: a partner announcing withdrawal, legislators changing position, seats becoming vacant, or an actual vote. Identify which event has occurred before predicting the outcome.

## The relevant House matters

Under Article 75(3), the Union Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to **Lok Sabha**. The corresponding state provision, Article 164(2), concerns the Legislative Assembly. Rajya Sabha’s important legislative and scrutiny roles do not make it the House that sustains a Union government through confidence. [1](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=65) [2](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=104)

A coalition combines support from more than one party. A party can also support a government without its members becoming ministers. For evaluating a particular government, ask for the stated support arrangements and the relevant proceedings; a photograph of leaders meeting is not a vote count.

## Majority of what?

Article 100(1) sets the ordinary rule of a majority of members **present and voting**, except where the Constitution provides otherwise. The presiding officer does not vote in the first instance and has a casting vote if votes are equal. Some parliamentary decisions have other requirements, so do not apply this rule to every constitutional vote. [3](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=75)

**Hypothetical example:** 300 members cast votes in an ordinary division, with 151 on one side and 149 on the other. The first side has more votes even if the House’s full membership is larger. Members who do not cast a vote are not votes for either side in that division. This arithmetic alone does not settle the lawfulness of an absence or abstention.

An election-results seat tally answers a different question from the number of votes cast on a later day. Vacancies, attendance, changed affiliations and disputed disqualifications must be handled using current records, not guessed from the previous election graphic.

## A confidence test is a proceeding

A confidence motion asks the House to affirm support; a no-confidence motion challenges the Council of Ministers’ continuation. Each has procedural requirements. Distinguish giving notice, admission, discussion and the result rather than reporting every notice as a government already having fallen. The constitutional background is collective responsibility, not social-media popularity. [1](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=65)

In reading a live situation, keep announcements and legal events on separate lines. “A party says it has withdrawn support” is a report about that announcement. “The government lost the division by these votes” requires the actual voting record.

## Losing support and dissolving the House are not synonyms

Appointment of a Prime Minister, the Council’s responsibility to Lok Sabha and dissolution of Lok Sabha involve different constitutional provisions. An early election should not be treated as automatically completed by a press conference or a disputed tally. [1](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=65) [4](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=71)

This page does not calculate a current majority. For that, record the date, House, vote, current membership issues and any relevant orders. A vote tracker should show unresolved questions rather than assigning contested members to whichever side makes the chart look decisive.

The useful habit is to ask two questions: “Which constitutional or procedural event are we describing?” and “What record establishes that it happened?”
