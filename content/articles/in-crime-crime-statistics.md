A crime statistic describes a defined set of recorded information. It is not automatically a count of every harmful event that occurred or every person who experienced harm.

Ask whether the source counts registered cases, victims, accused people, arrests, trials or outcomes. Those units cannot be substituted for one another.

## A real example of different categories

In an answer dated 12 August 2026, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways distinguished 2024 road fatalities from deaths in a criminal negligence category. It reported 177,175 fatalities in the MoRTH series, 175,142 road-accident deaths in NCRB's accidental-death series and 162,500 deaths in the Crime in India negligence category. [1](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2820_vMN9Gb.pdf?source=pqars#page=1)

The answer explains differences in reporting channels and the scope of accidents versus offences recorded under penal law. The explanation is the Ministry's account; it is not an independent audit of the three full datasets. The figures nevertheless illustrate why a series title and definition matter. [2](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/271/AU2820_vMN9Gb.pdf?source=pqars#page=2)

## A larger recorded count needs interpretation

In a **hypothetical district**, registered complaints rise after a reporting channel becomes easier to access. That rise alone cannot tell you how much underlying harm changed. You would need evidence about both events and reporting.

The reverse also needs care. A smaller recorded count is not automatic proof of improved safety. Do not invent a reporting explanation to dismiss a genuine deterioration either; investigate it.

## Do not mix a pipeline with a cohort

Suppose a hypothetical table reports cases registered during a year and convictions delivered during that year. The judgments may concern cases registered in earlier years. Dividing one by the other does not automatically produce the conviction rate for that year's new cases.

Read the publisher's stated denominator and follow the same cohort when that is the question you want to answer.

## “Political crime” needs a definition

An offence allegedly involving a politician, election violence and a claim of politically motivated prosecution are different categories. Decide what belongs in a collection and what evidence establishes a political connection.

Party affiliation alone does not establish motive. Nor does an accused person's assertion that a case is political settle whether the allegation is true or false.

Keep incident data, case status and interpretations of motive in separate fields. Where the underlying table or current proceeding cannot be verified, leave the claim unestablished rather than manufacturing a national total.
