# Why should I care about politics?

You do not have to enjoy political arguments to care about the decisions being made for you. Start with something concrete: the road outside your home, a public hospital, or what happens to money collected in taxes.

These are not all controlled by the same person. India's Constitution divides lawmaking subjects between the Union and states, while state laws determine important powers of local bodies. Finding the responsible institution is more useful than blaming whichever politician is most visible. [1](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=176) [2](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf#page=162)

## Start with a problem, not a party

Suppose your street floods whenever it rains. Ask who owns the road, who maintains its drains, what work was authorised and whether that work happened. A photograph records the flooding. A work order may identify the job and contractor. A payment record answers a different question: whether money was paid.

This is a **hypothetical example**, not a report about a particular street. It shows why political awareness includes reading documents and following decisions, not just watching speeches.

## Follow the money beyond the announcement

A budget is a place to start. The Union Budget publishes separate documents for proposed spending, revenue and other financial information. Its tables distinguish estimates from actual expenditure. An amount announced for a service does not, on its own, establish that the service reached you. [3](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Key_to_Budget_Document_2026.pdf#page=2)

Treat delivery as a separate question. Was the hospital constructed? Is it staffed? Are the promised services available? The evidence needed for one answer may not settle the others.

## You can begin without following everything

Choose one issue you already encounter. Identify the institution involved. Read one original record. Then ask a question that could produce a useful answer: “Which office is responsible for this work, and where is its latest progress report?”

You can support a policy and still question its implementation. You can disagree with a party and still recognise a well-supported result. The standard should be whether the claim survives examination.

The first goal is modest: replace “politicians do everything” or “nothing can change” with a clearer account of who made a decision, what happened next and what evidence is missing.

## Try this

Pick one recurring problem. Write down the decision you want explained, the likely responsible body and one record that would help.
