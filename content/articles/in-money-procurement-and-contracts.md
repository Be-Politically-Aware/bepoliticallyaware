“The contract was awarded” does not tell you whether the work was completed or whether the delivered goods met the requirements. Procurement scrutiny starts by identifying the stage of the process, not by assuming every document describes the finished result.

## Which rulebook applies?

The Department of Expenditure maintains separate manuals for goods, works, consultancy and non-consultancy services. Its current directory lists a **Works Second Edition 2025** entry dated **5 January 2026**, alongside the **Goods Second Edition 2024** and 2025 service manuals. A guide should not present the older works manual as the only current edition. [1](https://doe.gov.in/en/manuals)

This page uses the central goods manual for the basic record trail. State rules, a municipal works contract or a specialised purchase may differ. Identify the purchasing authority and applicable tender conditions before making a legal allegation.

## Follow the stages

The goods manual’s procurement cycle separates need assessment, bid invitation, evaluation and award, contract management and disposal. Its disclosure provisions concern tender notices, changes and award information. This provides a useful map of records to look for, rather than a promise that every file is publicly available without exception. [2](https://www.iitk.ac.in/centralstores/data/Manual_Goods_2024-17-09-24.pdf#page=66)

**Hypothetical example:** a department buys classroom computers. The tender describes what is required. A bid contains a supplier’s offer. The award identifies the selected supplier and terms. Delivery and inspection records address what arrived and whether it was accepted. A payment record addresses money paid. None of these alone establishes that children can use working computers during lessons.

Keep the tender number and contract identifier with your notes. Similar project titles can refer to different lots or years. Check whether a corrigendum changed the specification or deadline before comparing bids.

## “Cheapest” is not a complete evaluation rule

The central goods manual describes recommendation of a qualified bidder with a substantially responsive, lowest evaluated bid. A low quoted number alone is not the whole test. Published specifications, eligibility and evaluation conditions matter. [3](https://www.iitk.ac.in/centralstores/data/Manual_Goods_2024-17-09-24.pdf#page=201)

A higher price can therefore be a question to investigate, not automatic proof of corruption. Ask whether the bids were actually comparable: quantity, taxes, delivery, maintenance, warranty and required performance can change what is being purchased. Conversely, a low price does not prove fair competition if the process or specification was improperly shaped.

## Find the award, then check delivery

The manual provides for publication of award details on relevant procurement platforms and departmental channels, with specified exceptions. An absent search result should not immediately become an allegation of concealment; first check the identifier, platform, dates and any applicable exception. [4](https://www.iitk.ac.in/centralstores/data/Manual_Goods_2024-17-09-24.pdf#page=203)

For a service failure, the strongest next question may concern delivery rather than the original tender: “Which inspection recorded acceptance, what defect was found, and what action followed?” A contract variation, delayed payment and failed inspection are distinct events and deserve separate entries.

Build a factual timeline before naming misconduct. Compare the stated rule with the documented departure, request an explanation and include a material response. An irregularity can be serious without being identical to a proven criminal offence.

Useful accountability does not stop at finding a large contract value. It asks whether the purchase was justified, the process met its applicable requirements, and the public received what was paid for.
