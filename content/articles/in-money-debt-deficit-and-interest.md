“The deficit fell” does not necessarily mean the government borrowed fewer rupees. It can mean that a deficit is smaller relative to GDP while its rupee amount has risen. Identify the measure before deciding what the headline means.

## Stock, flow and denominator

A **debt stock** is an amount outstanding at a specified date. A **deficit** concerns the gap in a specified period. **Interest payments** are a cost associated with obligations, not the entire outstanding debt. Mixing these measures is like comparing a balance at a date with a year’s movement without saying which is which.

Budget at a Glance defines fiscal deficit using expenditure and receipts excluding debt-capital receipts. It distinguishes this from **revenue deficit**, the gap between revenue expenditure and revenue receipts, and **primary deficit**, fiscal deficit minus interest payments. The categories answer different questions. [1](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=1)

## A real example of rupees rising while the ratio falls

The Union Budget table shows **FY 2025–26 Revised Estimates** of fiscal deficit at **₹15,58,492 crore, or 4.4% of GDP**. For **FY 2026–27 Budget Estimates**, it shows **₹16,95,768 crore, or 4.3% of GDP**. The rupee estimate rises while the reported GDP ratio falls. These are different financial years and different estimate stages, not two completed actual outcomes. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

The table also gives a nominal GDP assumption for FY 2026–27. A ratio’s denominator can grow; the ratio cannot be interpreted by looking only at its numerator. Do not describe a nominal GDP assumption as real growth after inflation. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

## Reproduce the primary deficit

For FY 2026–27 BE, the table lists interest payments of **₹14,03,972 crore**. Subtracting that from the fiscal deficit gives **₹2,91,796 crore**, the listed primary deficit. This calculation removes interest for this measure; it does not make interest disappear from the government’s obligations. [2](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/bag1.pdf#page=3)

The supplied dataset preserves reported deficit ratios and the original rupee figures. Small differences can occur when reproducing other subtotals because the source warns that published values are rounded. Do not “correct” the source figures just to force an exact sum.

## What the number cannot decide for you

A deficit ratio alone does not show the quality of services, the usefulness of an investment, the reliability of revenue assumptions or who bears a spending cut. Those are separate questions. A smaller deficit achieved by postponing necessary work is different from one achieved by eliminating waste, even when a headline number looks similar.

**Hypothetical example:** two governments report the same annual deficit. One uses resources for a functioning water network; another pays for assets that are never used. The deficit is relevant to financing, but outcome evidence is needed to distinguish the programmes.

Ask what is included, which level of government is covered, what risks sit outside the quoted number and whether actual results later matched the estimate. Do not add Union and state debt figures indiscriminately if that would count the same intergovernmental obligation twice.

A good summary names the stock or flow, the date or year, and the denominator. “Debt is bad” and “deficits do not matter” are both substitutes for that work.
