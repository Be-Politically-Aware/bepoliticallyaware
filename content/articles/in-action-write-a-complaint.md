# How do I write a useful public-service complaint?

A useful complaint is specific enough for another person to investigate without guessing what you mean. Its strength comes from the facts and requested action, not the number of people copied into it.

First identify the responsible service authority. If using CPGRAMS, check that the issue fits its published scope and exclusions. A public-service grievance is different from an RTI request or a court proceeding. [1](https://pgportal.gov.in/)

## Use a short factual structure

The following is a **template**, not a report about a real incident:

> Subject: Repeated interruption of [service] at [location]
>
> I am reporting [specific problem] at [location or service reference]. I observed it on [dates and times].
>
> The attached [photographs, earlier complaint or other record] shows [limited factual description]. My earlier reference, if relevant, is [reference and date].
>
> Please investigate the issue, confirm the responsible office and explain the action taken or expected next step. If this is outside your office's responsibility, please identify the appropriate authority where known.
>
> Please provide a reference number or acknowledgement so I can follow up.

Do not add invented damage, injuries or previous conversations to make the complaint sound more serious. Separate your observations from information supplied by someone else.

## Attach only what helps

Keep originals privately and submit a focused set of relevant records. Label photographs with date and location, while being clear when those details rely on your own account.

Remove unrelated identity information from anything you publish publicly. Do not include neighbours' phone numbers, a child's identity or private medical details merely because they appear in a screenshot.

## Follow the actual response

CPGRAMS describes tracking through a registration ID and a feedback-linked appeal facility. Where you use that system, follow the current portal instructions rather than assuming every complaint channel has identical options. [2](https://pgportal.gov.in/)

Keep four events separate: submission, acknowledgement, response and observed outcome. “Closed” may tell you that a process ended; read the reason and check whether it addresses your original issue.

## Escalate the gap, not the volume

A useful follow-up says: “The response refers to [X], but my complaint concerns [Y]. Please address [specific unanswered point].” Repeating the entire complaint in more aggressive language may leave the same factual ambiguity unresolved.

Never treat this template as a substitute for an urgent safety response or a legal appeal with its own requirements.

## Try this

Draft a complaint in under 200 words. Check that another person can identify the location, dates, evidence and action requested without asking you to explain it again.
